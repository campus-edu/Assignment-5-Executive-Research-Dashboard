Executive Summary & Approach

This multi-jurisdictional research was conducted to evaluate the legal, operational, and financial feasibility of QuickBite Technologies expanding into three target states over the next 18 months. The goal is identifying compliance friction points, estimating unit fulfillment impacts, and recommending the optimal beachhead for Series A capital allocation ($2.0M uncommitted balance).

2

State Selection Logic

We selected three jurisdictions representing distinct regulatory climates: Texas represents a business-friendly, common-law independent contractor sanctuary; Florida represents a high-growth secondary market with county-level tax complexity; California represents a heavily regulated benchmark governed by Proposition 22 and municipal fee caps, serving as our stress-test control.

3

Primary Research Pillars & Data Sources

All legal and statutory claims are grounded in Tier 1 government repositories (.gov):

  • Labor Codes: Tex. Lab. Code § 201.041; Fla. Stat. § 443.036; Cal. B&PC § 7451 (Prop 22; Castellanos, 2024).
  • Food Safety: 25 TAC § 228 (TFER); Tex. Health Code Ch. 437; Fla. Stat. Ch. 500/509; CalCode § 113700+.
  • Taxes & Fees: Tex. Tax Code § 151.0242; Fla. Stat. § 212.0596; Cal. Rev. & Tax § 6041; SF Police Art. 53.
4

Analytical Ranking Framework

Jurisdictional business-friendliness was scored out of 10 using an asymmetric operational weighting model: Driver Classification (45%) reflects our primary unit cost driver ($4.85 TX vs. $7.95 CA); Business Ops & Taxes (35%) evaluates fee caps and filing friction; Food Safety & Licensing (20%) assesses courier onboarding speed and bag seals.

Assumptions & Operational Limitations

Limitations: Analysis focuses on state-level statutes and primary municipal ordinances; ongoing constitutional litigation surrounding Texas HB 2127 requires dynamic docket monitoring prior to Phase 2 rollout.
